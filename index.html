Committee Procedure in Practice

How debate works

Open Debate

Delegates may speak from any position on the question before the committee. You do not have to be assigned to a “for” or “against” side.

You may hearThe floor is open for debate.

Closed Debate

Speaking time is divided between those in favour and those against. PyleaMUN normally uses this structure for amendments when a clear comparison of positions is useful.

You may hearWe will hear speeches in favour, followed by speeches against.

Moderated Caucus

A focused series of short formal speeches on one subtopic. You remain in formal session and speak only when recognised.

How to move for oneThe delegation of Greece moves for a 10-minute Moderated Caucus on media verification, with a speaking time of 45 seconds.

Unmoderated Caucus

Formal speaking order is paused so delegates can negotiate, merge ideas, draft text and build support.

How to move for oneThe delegation of Greece moves for a 15-minute Unmoderated Caucus for resolution drafting.

Points — use the problem to choose the point

I cannot participate properly

Point of Personal Privilege

Use it for an immediate condition such as not being able to hear. It may interrupt only if immediate correction is necessary.

I think procedure is wrong

Point of Order

Use it to identify a procedural error. Keep it brief and do not argue the substance of the topic.

I do not know what to do next

Point of Parliamentary Inquiry

Ask the Chair how the procedure works or what action is currently in order.

I want to question the speaker

Point of Information to the Speaker

Ask one concise question after the speech, if the speaker accepts questions.

I need a factual or administrative clarification

Point of Information to the Chair

Use this where no more specific point applies.

Useful safety phrasePoint of Parliamentary Inquiry: would it be in order to…?

Support is not a promise. Supporting the introduction of a Draft Resolution means you agree that it should be discussed. It does not require you to vote for it in the final vote.

Amendments — change exact wording

An amendment changes an introduced Draft Resolution by adding, deleting or replacing specified wording. Always identify the exact location and the exact change.

Encourages Member States to establish media-literacy programmes in secondary education;

ADD

Add words, a phrase or a new clause at a precise location.

DELETE

Remove specified wording or a specified clause.

REPLACE

Substitute exact wording with proposed new wording.

Non-controversial amendments: the Chair may ask whether there is an objection to a technical or plainly non-controversial amendment. If nobody objects, it may be incorporated without extended debate. Any objection returns it to ordinary amendment procedure.

Voting — the essentials

Procedural vote

  • Decides how the committee will work.
  • No abstentions.
  • Simple majority unless a rule says otherwise.

Substantive vote

  • Decides the substance of a proposal.
  • Usually: in favour, against or abstain.
  • “Present and Voting” removes the option to abstain.
Simple majority: more votes in favour than against. Abstentions are not counted on either side. A tie does not adopt the proposal.

What PyleaMUN expects from you

Debate ideas, not people

Use diplomatic language and treat delegates, Student Officers, staff, teachers and guests with respect.

Use technology responsibly

Devices are for legitimate conference work when permitted. Outside communication must stop during voting.

Protect academic integrity

Do not misrepresent externally produced material as your own. Follow any additional PyleaMUN guidance on AI and collaborative drafting.

Safety overrides procedure. In an emergency, committee business stops immediately and participants follow the directions of responsible adults and emergency authorities.

Rules of Procedure

⌕
I

Foundations

Authority, scope, interpretation, language and parliamentary courtesy.

Rule 1 Scope of the Rules

These Rules govern standard PyleaMUN committees. Appendix A adds rules for the Security Council. A specialized committee may operate under a separate handbook issued or approved by the Secretariat.

Where a specialized handbook is silent, these Rules apply so far as they are compatible with that committee's format.

Rule 2 Procedural Authority

The Chairpersons are responsible for applying these Rules during committee sessions. They may make reasonable procedural rulings needed to preserve fairness, relevance and the orderly progress of debate.

A Chair's discretion does not permit the creation of a substantive voting right or the removal of a voting right established by these Rules.

Rule 3 Interpretation and Unprovided Situations

If a situation is not expressly covered, the Chair shall use the closest applicable rule and the general principles of fair participation and efficient debate. Important or disputed questions may be referred to the Secretary-General or the Secretariat member responsible for committees.

The Secretary-General's procedural determination is final for the conference.

Rule 4 Official Language

English is the working language of PyleaMUN. Speeches, motions, points, formal written submissions and committee communication shall be in English unless the Secretariat authorizes an exception.

Rule 5 Parliamentary Courtesy

Delegates shall address the committee through the Chair, refer to other participants by delegation or official role, and maintain diplomatic language. The Chair may call a speaker to order for personal, insulting, discriminatory or deliberately disruptive remarks.

II

Starting and Managing a Session

Opening, Roll Call, quorum, recognition and Chairing discretion.

Rule 6 Opening of a Committee Session

The Chair opens each session, announces the business before the committee and identifies any procedural instructions that apply to that session.

Rule 7 Roll Call

At the first session of the day, and at other times when necessary, the Chair shall call delegations. A voting delegation answers 'Present' or 'Present and Voting'.

A delegation recorded as 'Present and Voting' may not abstain on substantive votes until a later roll call changes that status.

Rule 8 Quorum

Formal substantive business requires at least one half of the registered voting delegations to be present. The Chair may conduct announcements, procedural explanations or informal organizational work before quorum is reached.

If attendance later falls below quorum, the Chair may pause substantive decision-making until quorum is restored.

Rule 9 Recognition and the Floor

No delegate may make a formal speech or motion without recognition by the Chair, except for an intervention that these Rules expressly allow to interrupt.

Recognition is not automatic. The Chair should distribute opportunities fairly while considering relevance, previous participation and the need for balanced debate.

Rule 10 Chairing Discretion

The Chair may set reasonable speaking times, limit repetitive interventions, combine substantially identical motions and rule dilatory or clearly irrelevant proposals out of order.

The Chair should explain a significant procedural ruling when an explanation would assist the committee.

III

Forms of Debate

Open Debate, Closed Debate, Speakers’ List and caucuses.

Rule 11 Open Debate

Open Debate is the ordinary PyleaMUN format. Delegates may speak from any position on the matter before the committee, subject to recognition and the announced speaking time.

Rule 12 Closed Debate

Closed Debate separates speaking time in favour from speaking time against. It is normally used for amendments and may be used for another question when a structured comparison of opposing positions would improve debate.

The Chair announces the order and duration of each side before debate begins.

Rule 13 Speakers' List

The committee may use a Speakers' List for sustained formal discussion. The Chair announces the speaking time and the method for joining or leaving the list.

A Speakers' List may be suspended while another debate format is in progress and resumed afterwards.

Rule 14 Moderated Caucus

A Moderated Caucus is a focused sequence of short speeches on a defined subtopic. Delegates remain in formal session and are recognized individually by the Chair.

A motion for a Moderated Caucus shall state its topic, total duration and proposed individual speaking time.

Rule 15 Unmoderated Caucus and Lobbying

An Unmoderated Caucus temporarily suspends formal speaking order so that delegates may negotiate, draft, merge proposals or organize support. Lobbying may also be scheduled by the conference as a dedicated drafting period.

The Chair announces the duration and purpose. Committee rules of conduct continue to apply throughout.

IV

Points, Replies and Questions

Interventions that help delegates participate, correct procedure and question speakers.

Rule 16 Point of Personal Privilege

A Point of Personal Privilege concerns a condition that materially prevents a delegate from participating, such as inability to hear the speaker or an immediate physical-access issue.

It may interrupt only when the problem requires immediate correction. It may never be used to respond to the substance of a speech.

Rule 17 Point of Order

A Point of Order identifies an alleged procedural error. The delegate should state the procedural issue briefly and without arguing the merits of the topic.

During voting, it is in order only when it concerns the conduct or accuracy of the voting procedure.

Rule 18 Point of Parliamentary Inquiry

A Point of Parliamentary Inquiry asks the Chair to explain a rule, the status of committee business or the correct procedural method for taking an action. It may not interrupt a speaker.

Rule 19 Point of Information to the Speaker

After a speech, and only if the speaker agrees to questions, the Chair may recognize Points of Information. Each point must be a concise question relevant to the speech.

A delegate may not use the question to deliver a speech, conduct a direct dialogue or make a rhetorical statement.

Rule 20 Point of Information to the Chair

A delegate may ask the Chair for factual or administrative clarification relevant to committee work when no more specific point applies. It may not interrupt a speaker.

Rule 21 Right of Reply

A Right of Reply may be requested after a statement that directly attacks the dignity of a delegate or seriously impugns the represented state or organization beyond ordinary policy criticism.

The request does not interrupt the speaker. If granted, the Chair sets a short response time. A Right of Reply may not itself generate another Right of Reply.

V

Motions and Changes of Procedure

How delegates ask the committee to change what happens next.

Rule 22 General Rules for Motions

Motions are raised when the floor is open. A motion must be sufficiently precise for the Chair and committee to understand the requested action.

The Chair may seek seconds or objections, compare competing motions, or rule a motion out of order if it is repetitive, impossible within the schedule or intended primarily to obstruct committee work.

Rule 23 Motion to Set the Agenda

When the order of multiple agenda items has not already been fixed, a delegate may move to establish which item will be considered first. The Chair may allow brief procedural arguments before a simple-majority vote.

Rule 24 Motion to Establish a Speakers' List

A delegate may move to open a Speakers' List and propose an individual speaking time. The Chair may adjust the time before putting the motion to a procedural vote.

Rule 25 Motion for a Moderated Caucus

The motion shall identify a focused topic, total duration and individual speaking time. If several such motions are proposed, the Chair determines their order by relevance and practical usefulness.

Rule 26 Motion for an Unmoderated Caucus

The motion shall state a purpose and duration. The Chair may shorten the proposed duration or reject the motion when informal negotiation would not assist the business currently before the committee.

Rule 27 Motion to Move to Open or Closed Debate

A delegate may request a change of debate format. The Chair may decide the matter directly where the change is purely managerial or may submit it to a procedural vote.

Rule 28 Motion to Limit or Extend Debate Time

A delegate may propose a specific change to an announced debate period or caucus. The Chair may modify the proposed duration before the motion is considered.

Unless the Chair treats the adjustment as a minor scheduling matter, adoption requires a two-thirds majority.

Rule 29 Motion to Move to the Previous Question

This motion closes the current stage of debate and moves the committee to the next required step, normally voting. In a divided Closed Debate, it may move the committee from the first side to the second side.

The Chair may proceed without a vote if there is no objection. If a vote is required, a two-thirds majority is necessary.

Rule 30 Motion to Divide the Question

Before the final vote on a Draft Resolution, a delegate may propose that specified operative clauses or clearly defined groups of operative clauses be voted on separately.

The motion must identify the proposed division exactly. If the division is approved by two-thirds, the separated portions are voted on before the remaining text.

Rule 31 Motion to Divide the House

After a substantive vote, the committee may be asked to repeat the vote without abstentions. The Chair shall entertain this motion only where a definitive binary result would serve a genuine procedural purpose.

If granted, every delegation entitled to vote must vote either in favour or against.

Rule 32 Motion to Reconsider

A substantive decision may be reopened only through a Motion to Reconsider. The motion is in order after the immediately pending business has concluded and may not be used repeatedly against the same decision.

Adoption requires a two-thirds majority.

Rule 33 Motion to Recess

A Motion to Recess temporarily suspends the meeting. The delegate should propose a duration. The Chair may accept, shorten, postpone or reject the recess according to the conference timetable.

Rule 34 Motion to Adjourn

Adjournment ends the committee's work for the conference. The motion is in order only when the scheduled substantive work has been completed or when the Secretariat authorizes early adjournment.

VI

Speaking Time and Yields

What happens to unused speaking time and how follow-up questions work.

Rule 35 Yield to Points of Information

A speaker may offer the remaining speaking time for questions. The Chair controls recognition and may stop questions when the available time has expired.

Rule 36 Yield to Another Delegate

A speaker may transfer unused speaking time to one other delegation if the Chair permits. The receiving delegate may speak for the remaining time but may not yield that time onward.

Rule 37 Yield to the Chair

A speaker may return unused time to the Chair. If a speaker finishes without making another permitted yield, the floor returns to the Chair automatically.

Rule 38 Follow-up Questions

After receiving an answer to a Point of Information, the questioner may ask the Chair for one follow-up. The follow-up must arise directly from the answer and may not introduce a separate issue.

Permission is entirely at the Chair's discretion.

VII

Working Papers and Draft Resolutions

From informal ideas to an official text before the committee.

Rule 39 Working Papers

A Working Paper is an informal written proposal used during negotiation or drafting. It may contain ideas, draft clauses, a policy outline or a developing full text.

Working Papers do not have the status of Draft Resolutions and are not voted on as final committee texts.

Rule 40 Drafting Standards

Formal texts shall use the format prescribed by PyleaMUN. Operative language should be clear, actionable and within the mandate of the committee. The Chair or designated approval team may return unclear or improperly formatted text for revision.

Rule 41 Main Submitter and Supporting Delegations

A proposed Draft Resolution shall identify a Main Submitter and may identify Co-Submitters. The Main Submitter is normally the delegation responsible for coordinating the text.

Before introduction, the proposal must demonstrate support for discussion from at least one quarter of the voting delegations present, unless the Secretariat announces a different threshold for that committee. Support for introduction does not require a favourable final vote.

Rule 42 Approval and Numbering

A document becomes an official Draft Resolution only after completing the conference's approval process and receiving an official designation. Before that point it remains a Working Paper or proposed text.

Rule 43 Introduction of a Draft Resolution

Once approved, the Draft Resolution may be introduced when recognized by the Chair. The Main Submitter may be asked to summarize its principal measures or identify key operative clauses before debate opens.

Rule 44 Merging Draft Texts

The Chair may encourage or direct compatible drafting groups to negotiate a merged text when this would reduce unnecessary duplication. A merged document must still complete the normal approval process.

Rule 45 Withdrawal of a Draft Text

Before final voting begins, the Main Submitter may request withdrawal of a Draft Resolution. The Chair shall consider whether the text has acquired sufficient independent support that the committee should retain it for consideration.

VIII

Amendments

How introduced Draft Resolutions may be changed.

Rule 46 Purpose and Form of Amendments

An amendment changes the text of an introduced Draft Resolution by adding, deleting or replacing specified wording. The submission must identify the exact location and proposed change.

Rule 47 Admissibility of Amendments

The Chair may reject an amendment that is unintelligible, outside the committee's mandate, unrelated to the Draft Resolution, duplicative of a pending amendment or inconsistent with an already adopted decision.

Rule 48 Introduction and Debate on Amendments

An accepted amendment is introduced by its submitter when recognized. Amendments are normally considered in Closed Debate so that the committee has an opportunity to hear both support and opposition.

Rule 49 Non-controversial Amendments

Where an amendment is technical or plainly non-controversial, the Chair may ask whether there is an objection. If no delegation objects, the amendment may be incorporated without extended debate. Any objection returns the amendment to the ordinary procedure.

IX

Voting

Voting procedure, voting rights and majority requirements.

Rule 50 Beginning of Voting Procedure

The Chair clearly announces when voting procedure begins. From that moment until the result is declared, delegates shall remain seated, outside communication shall stop and entry or exit shall be restricted except for an emergency.

Rule 51 Voting Rights

Each voting delegation has one vote. No delegation may vote for another. Invited observers or participants without voting status may not vote.

Rule 52 Procedural Votes

Procedural questions do not permit abstentions. Unless a specific rule establishes another threshold, a procedural motion is adopted by a simple majority.

Rule 53 Substantive Votes

On substantive questions, a delegation may vote in favour, against or abstain unless it is recorded as 'Present and Voting' or abstentions have been excluded by an applicable procedure.

Unless a specific rule or committee appendix provides otherwise, a substantive proposal is adopted by a simple majority of votes cast in favour and against.

Rule 54 Simple Majority

A simple majority exists when votes in favour exceed votes against. Abstentions are not counted on either side of that comparison. A tie does not adopt the proposal.

Rule 55 Two-Thirds Majority

Where these Rules require two-thirds, at least two-thirds of the votes counted for that question must support the proposal. The Chair shall announce the required number when useful.

Rule 56 Roll Call Vote

The Chair may order a Roll Call Vote where individual voting records are appropriate. Delegations are called one at a time. A delegation may pass once and shall be called again before the vote closes.

Rule 57 Explanation of Vote

During a Roll Call Vote, the Chair may permit a brief explanation of an unusual vote. The explanation occurs after the result and may not reopen debate.

X

Conduct and Conference Administration

Respect, integrity, technology, safety, sustainability and disciplinary response.

Rule 58 Professional Conduct

Participants shall treat one another, Student Officers, staff, teachers and guests with respect. Harassment, discriminatory conduct, threats, deliberate humiliation and persistent disruption are prohibited.

Rule 59 Academic Integrity

Delegates are responsible for the integrity of the material they submit or present as their own. Sources should be used responsibly, and externally produced material should not be misrepresented as original delegate work.

The Secretariat may establish additional rules for the use of artificial-intelligence tools, pre-written material or collaborative drafting.

Rule 60 Dress and Identification

Participants shall follow the conference's formal-dress guidance and wear official identification where required for access or security.

Rule 61 Electronic Devices

Electronic devices may be used for legitimate conference work when permitted by the Chair. They must remain silent and must not be used for unauthorized communication during voting.

Rule 62 Conference Property

Placards, gavels, flags, signs, technical equipment and other conference or school property shall be used carefully and only for their intended purpose.

Rule 63 Safety

In an emergency, procedural business stops immediately. Participants shall follow the directions of school personnel, the Organizing Team and emergency authorities. Student Officers shall not delay safety instructions in order to complete debate or voting.

Rule 64 Prohibited Conduct on Conference Premises

Participants must comply with school rules and applicable law. Conduct or possession of items prohibited by the host institution or law may be referred immediately to responsible adults and the Organizing Team.

Rule 65 Sustainability

PyleaMUN encourages digital circulation of documents, responsible printing and proper disposal or recycling of materials. The Organizing Team may issue additional sustainability instructions.

Rule 66 Disciplinary Response

For procedural misconduct, the Chair may issue a warning, withdraw recognition temporarily or refer the matter to the Secretariat. Serious non-procedural conduct shall be handled by the responsible conference and school authorities.

XI

Final Authority

Knowledge of the rules, temporary adjustment and final procedural authority.

Rule 67 Knowledge of the Rules

Delegates and Student Officers are expected to know the core procedures relevant to their role. Chairs should nevertheless explain unfamiliar procedure when doing so can prevent confusion without unfairly assisting one delegation.

Rule 68 Temporary Procedural Adjustment

The Secretary-General may authorize a temporary adjustment to procedure when necessary because of timetable constraints, technology failure, emergency conditions or the design of a particular committee.

Any material adjustment should be announced clearly to the affected participants.

Rule 69 Final Procedural Authority

The Secretary-General is the final procedural authority of PyleaMUN. Nothing in these Rules prevents the Secretariat from taking reasonable action required for the safety, fairness or effective administration of the conference.

Security Council — What Changes?

If you are not serving in a Security Council committee, you can skip this section. The general Rules apply unless Appendix A provides a different Security Council procedure.

Security Council procedure: the Security Council may use a more flexible debate format and substantive decisions are subject to the P5 veto system announced for the simulation.
SC

Appendix A — Security Council

SC Rule 1 Relationship with the General Rules

The general Rules apply unless this Appendix provides a different Security Council procedure.

SC Rule 2 Debate Format

The Security Council may use direct formal recognition, speaking rounds, Moderated Caucuses and Unmoderated Caucuses without maintaining a permanent Speakers' List. The Chair selects the format best suited to the issue before the Council.

SC Rule 3 Agenda Management

The Council considers the agenda announced by the Secretariat. Urgent developments may be introduced or reordered only with authorization from the Secretariat.

SC Rule 4 Participation of Non-Council Members

A state or other participant directly concerned by the matter may be invited to address the Council. An invited non-member may participate in debate as authorized by the Chair but does not receive a Security Council vote.

SC Rule 5 Questions to Invited Participants

The Chair may establish a dedicated question period for an invited participant. Questions remain under the Chair's control and must concern the matter for which the participant was invited.

SC Rule 6 Procedural Voting

The veto does not apply to procedural questions. The Chair shall announce the procedural threshold used by the PyleaMUN Security Council before formal voting begins.

SC Rule 7 Substantive Voting and the Permanent Members

Substantive decisions are subject to the Security Council voting system announced for the simulation. A negative substantive vote by a Permanent Member - China, France, the Russian Federation, the United Kingdom or the United States - constitutes a veto. An abstention by a Permanent Member is not a veto.

SC Rule 8 P5 Consultation

Where a veto has occurred or appears likely, the Chair may authorize a short consultation among the Permanent Members. The consultation does not suspend the rights of the full Council longer than necessary and its procedural outcome shall be reported when debate resumes.

SC Rule 9 Priority of this Appendix

If a general rule and a Security Council rule cannot both operate, the Security Council rule controls.
